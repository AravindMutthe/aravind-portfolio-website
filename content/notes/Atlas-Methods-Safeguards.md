# Atlas Notes — Research Methods, Ontology and Safeguards

This note is the Atlas method, in one place, so the curriculum can be read critically instead of taken on trust.

## Research ontology and cross-links

### Communications

Couriers and semaphore, electrical telegraph, telephone and broadcast, computer packet networks, TCP/IP Internet, mobile and cloud and edge, AI-assisted networks.

### Network engineering

For each era, examine: media, encoding, addressing, switching and routing, protocols, devices, ownership, operations, regulation, security, storage, pricing and user interfaces.

### Institutional and business systems

Contracts, trade, banks, railways, ledgers, enterprise software, procurement, regulatory oversight and global supply chains.

### Operating systems

Serial TTY, NIC drivers, kernel network stack, namespaces, routing, netfilter, IPC, schedulers, memory and storage, with analogy limits clearly stated.

### Architecture

Monolithic versus modular versus distributed versus service-oriented and programmable networking are independent architectural choices, not a one-way historical succession.

### AI

Distinguish three different things that are often blurred into one phrase:

- AI traffic over ordinary networks.
- AI-managed network operations.
- Communication among AI agents.

Retain human approval and auditing for consequential actions in all three.

## Research paths in the Atlas

- Communications history, then government and standards, then NIC and TCP/IP, then kernel and operating system, then client and server protocols, then monorepo and cloud deployment, then business processes and contracts.
- Or, as a learning path: engineering and interviews — hardware, NIC, drivers, kernel, TCP/IP, systems and operations.
- Or: history, business and founders — trade and finance, communications, institutions, industry supply chains, business strategy.

## Evidence and accuracy safeguards

- A prior assistant historical claim is a record of that answer, not independent verification of the claim.
- ARPA, DARPA and ARPANET must be distinguished. ARPANET was a research packet network, not synonymous with the entire Internet.
- The assertion that the telegraph was the first business network is an oversimplification. Older courier, postal, maritime and merchant networks predate it.
- Linux source paths and network feature support are version and configuration dependent.
- Historical analogies such as business departments compared with VLANs, or government branches compared with kernel subsystems, are pedagogical metaphors, not structural identities.
- Exact conversation dates in the Atlas identify recovered conversations. Exact user wording and full message boundaries are not established for every entry. Do not label them verbatim.
- A complete account export is required for full transcript fidelity. Until then, no exhaustive-coverage claim is warranted.

## Deduplication: what was removed, and why

The Atlas folder, as found, contained 238 files totalling 1.5 GB. By exact file hash, only 191 were unique. The rest were copies:

- The 20 handwritten notebook pages appear three times each, in blueprint, editorial and hybrid folders: 40 extra copies.
- The large offline Atlas HTML appears three times under different names: 2 extra copies.
- The expanded offline HTML appears twice: 1 extra copy.
- The era timeline README appears twice: 1 extra copy.
- The priority HTML appears twice, once at the top level and once as a start page: 1 extra copy.
- A download zip appears twice under different names: 1 extra copy.
- The recovery HTML appears twice, once at the top level and once inside the priority folder: 1 extra copy.

That accounts for 47 duplicate files. Beyond exact duplicates, the twelve large HTML exports share the same chapter text, with most of their size coming from embedded base64 images. Only one copy of that text was used.

Zip archives in the folder mostly repackage the same small text files and the same large HTML. Their small text contents were read, including additional dated context, verified history, recovered message evidence and project data, and their unique items are included in the evidence register. The archives themselves were not published.

## What was not published, and why

- 129 timestamped images at the top level of the Atlas folder, each about 3.3 MB, with no captions and no link to any chapter or note in the Atlas indexes. They were not published, because uncaptioned and unreviewed images cannot be safely described or attributed.
- 20 handwritten notebook scans, about 6 MB in total in their deduplicated form. They were not embedded in this first public version, because the Atlas itself says final chapter assignment requires line-by-line review, and the prototype is not fact-checked or published. They remain in the private Atlas archive for review.
- Private employment, company-formation, immigration and visa material, excluded by the Atlas inclusion criteria.
- Duplicate HTML exports, zip archives and download test files, which carry no unique content.

## Gaps and requirements for a verbatim complete export

- Exact full text of all historical user and assistant messages, with per-message IDs, timestamps and roles.
- Original attachments, diagrams, code and repository snapshots.
- Full coverage of August 2025, December 2025 and January 2026.
- Deduplication and traceability from each Atlas item to a conversation ID and source file.
- A ChatGPT account data export, to verify the missing records. Until then, no exhaustive-coverage claim is warranted, and this public version does not make one.
