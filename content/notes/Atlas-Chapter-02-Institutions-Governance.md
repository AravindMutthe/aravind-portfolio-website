# Atlas Chapter 02 — Who Builds and Governs Networks

Curriculum chapter 02. Theme: institutions and governance. Source pages in the Atlas: pages 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 11, 12, 13 and 14, provisionally assigned.

## The original note, in brief

The pages repeatedly connect standards bodies, regulators, business owners, vendors and customers. Names that recur in the notes include FCC, ICANN, RIR, IETF, IEEE and 3GPP, alongside business owners, vendors and customers. The text here is a topic-level extract, not a verbatim transcription.

## Understand each relationship

- Government: creates and enforces applicable legal requirements, and may also procure and operate networks.
- Standards bodies: publish technical specifications to support interoperability.
- Manufacturers and operators: build devices, deploy infrastructure and deliver services.
- Customers and users: the people and businesses whose demand, contracts and tariffs shape what gets built.

## Enhanced learning flow

A public need leads to rules and standards, which lead to vendors and operators, which lead to deployed networks, which reach customers. Again, this is a learning flow, not a claim that history always ran in that order.

## Ownership across eras

- Telegraph: private companies, government telegraphs, leased circuits, office codes, privacy rules.
- Telephone and radio: national operators, regulators, spectrum, licensing.
- Mainframe and ARPANET: research governance, universities, government research funding, later commercial ISPs and backbone operators.
- Internet: a mix of standards bodies, regional registries, domain governance, ISPs, cloud providers and hyperscalers.
- Cloud, mobile and AI: platform ownership, contracts, data and model governance, cybersecurity regulation.

The Atlas asks, for each era: who owned, funded, regulated, operated and consumed the network, and how were access, contracts and tariffs negotiated?

## Business and institutional systems

The same chapter connects to enterprise systems: telegraph and ledgers, then mainframes and MRP, then MRP II and ERP, then cloud ERP and SaaS, then post-ERP and AI. Compare management hierarchy, public regulation, storage, publishing, security, device exchange, business models and accounting in each step.

## What to watch out for

- Governance is not one thing. Technical standards, spectrum, competition, privacy and security are governed by different bodies, in different countries, at different times.
- Ownership and control are not the same. A network can be publicly funded, privately operated, and governed by a third set of standards bodies.
- Equity and intellectual property depend on contracts, not just on who built a device.
