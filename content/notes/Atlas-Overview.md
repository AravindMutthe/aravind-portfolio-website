# Systems Knowledge Atlas — Overview

A research notebook turned into a curriculum. The Atlas traces one question across eras: how do people, businesses and machines coordinate at a distance, and what changes when the medium changes?

## The question

- What was the communication medium, addressing method, switching or routing model, and physical device in each era?
- Who owned, funded, regulated, operated and consumed the network? How were access, contracts and tariffs negotiated?
- Which functions were monolithic, modular, interoperable, virtualized, programmable or AI-assisted? These are architecture choices, not a one-way historical succession.
- How did interfaces, protocols, identities, storage, operating systems, hardware, semiconductor supply chains and software evolve together?
- How do historical ideas map to modern Linux and kernel networking without equating an analogy with a mechanism?
- Which functions can AI automate, and which still require human authorization, governance, audit and control?

## The structure

The Atlas is published here in three layers, so nothing is lost and nothing is overstated:

- Curriculum — six sample chapters, plus a seventh chapter on government, business and technical networks, following the Atlas chapter order.
- Notes — the era timeline, the dated evidence register, and the engineering notes that sit behind the curriculum.
- Blogs — each chapter and note is also a standalone post, so it can be read in order or on its own.

## Two learning paths

- Engineering and interviews: hardware, NIC, drivers, kernel, TCP/IP, systems and operations.
- History, business and founders: trade and finance, communications, institutions, industry supply chains, business strategy.

## What this is, and what it is not

This is a summary of recovered research, not a verbatim transcript archive. The source material covers August to November 2025 in dated form. December 2025 and January 2026 are not verified, and August 1 to 27 is incomplete. No missing message text has been invented, and no summary here should be cited as an exact quote from an original conversation.

Original handwritten notes are the authoritative source for the curriculum chapters. Their chapter assignments in the Atlas are provisional: the Atlas itself says final assignment requires line-by-line source review. Enhanced explanations, analogies and corrections are always labelled as added material, not as original notes.

## What was left out, on purpose

- Private employment communication: internship and onboarding emails are not reproduced, and no private details from them appear here.
- Personal, immigration and company-formation topics that appear in some recovered fragments: these were excluded by the Atlas inclusion criteria and are not part of this public curriculum.
- Uncaptioned image files and duplicate exports: the Atlas folder contains many byte-identical copies of the same HTML pages, notebook scans and archives. Only one copy of each unique text was used. Duplicate files, zip archives and uncaptioned timestamp images were not published.
- The 20 handwritten notebook scans: they are not embedded in this first public version, because they have not yet had a line-by-line review for publication. They remain in the private Atlas archive. If they are reviewed and approved, they can be added as a source archive later.

## Accuracy safeguards

- A prior assistant answer is a record of that answer, not independent verification of its claim.
- ARPA, DARPA and ARPANET must be distinguished. ARPANET was a research packet-switched network, not synonymous with the entire Internet.
- The telegraph was not the first business network. Older courier, postal, maritime and merchant networks predate it.
- Linux source paths and network feature support vary by version and configuration.
- Analogies such as business departments compared with VLANs, or government branches compared with kernel subsystems, are teaching metaphors, not structural identities.
- "AI network" is not a single defined protocol family. Distinguish AI workloads running over networks, AI-assisted network operations, and communication among AI services and agents.

## Gaps that remain

- A complete account export, with per-message IDs, timestamps, roles and attachments, would be needed to verify wording and to close the December 2025 to January 2026 gap.
- Full coverage of August 2025 is not established.
- The curriculum chapters are samples, not a finished book. More chapters were planned as notes arrived.

Read the chapters in order, or start with the era timeline and the evidence register to see exactly what is dated, what is summarized, and what is still missing.
