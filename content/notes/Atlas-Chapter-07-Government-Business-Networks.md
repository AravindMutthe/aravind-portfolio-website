# Atlas Chapter 07 — Government, Business and Technical Networks

This is the seventh chapter in the wider Atlas editions, beyond the six sample chapters in the prototype curriculum. It is included here so the Atlas structure is not truncated.

## The idea

Government, business and technical networks are not three separate histories. They are three views of the same systems:

- Government networks: public communication paths, regulators, procurement, tenders, spectrum, standards and public-private partnerships.
- Business networks: trade, finance, enterprise systems, supply chains, contracts and customers.
- Technical networks: devices, protocols, kernels, storage, cloud and AI systems.

A telegraph line, for example, is simultaneously a government-regulated asset, a business service with tariffs and customers, and a technical system with wires, codes, operators and switching.

## Cross-era questions, applied

- Medium and addressing: couriers and postal addresses, Morse and office codes, telephone numbers, IP addresses, domain names, API identities, workload identities.
- Switching and routing: human operators, electromechanical switches, packet switches, routers, software-defined networks, orchestration.
- Ownership and finance: state telegraphs, chartered companies, national operators, ISPs, hyperscalers, platform companies.
- Regulation: postal rules, telecom regulation, spectrum pricing, competition, privacy, cybersecurity, AI governance.
- Supply: chip vendors, device manufacturers, cable and satellite operators, cloud capacity, energy.

## Network family tree

Telegraph, telephone, radio, TV, ARPANET, Internet, mobile from 1G to 5G, satellite, cloud, social media, IoT, AI, and speculative 6G and quantum networks. This is the family tree artifact recovered in the Atlas, October 2, 2025. It is a taxonomy, not a claim that each member replaced the previous one.

## Modular versus monolithic, again

This chapter is where the Atlas most explicitly corrects a simple story. Modular and monolithic are design choices, not epochs:

- A telegraph network can be modular: interchangeable physical wires, encoding, switching, ledger and delivery modules, with standards and APIs of their time.
- A modern cloud system can be monolithic: a single deployable unit, a single database, a single control plane.
- Distributed, service-oriented and programmable networking are further choices, independent of era.

## What to watch out for

- Do not read history backwards from the present. AI, cloud and mobile did not make older governance questions disappear; they moved them.
- Do not treat government, business and technology as separate chapters in real life. A tender decision is a technical decision, and a protocol decision is a business decision.
- Analogies have limits. Comparing kernel subsystems with government branches, or business departments with network zones, can help learning, but it does not prove the systems work the same way.
